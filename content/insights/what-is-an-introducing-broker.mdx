An **Introducing Broker (IB)** is an individual or business that refers clients to a trading broker and earns an ongoing commission — usually called a **rebate** — based on how much those clients trade. Unlike a one-time referral bonus, an IB's income recurs for as long as their referred clients keep trading.

The model exists because brokers compete heavily for traders, and word-of-mouth referrals from a trusted source — a trading educator, a community leader, a fellow trader — convert better than most other acquisition channels. Brokers pay for that trust by sharing a slice of the trading volume it brings in.

## How the IB model actually works

The mechanics are simpler than most people expect:

1. **You get a unique referral link or code** from the broker's IB programme.
2. **You share it** with your audience — a trading community, a Telegram channel, a YouTube following, a personal network of traders.
3. **Someone signs up through your link** and opens a live trading account with the broker.
4. **They trade.** Every time they open and close a position, that trade generates a small amount of spread or commission for the broker.
5. **You receive a rebate** — a fixed or tiered amount per lot traded — for the life of that client's account, not just their first trade.

That last point is what separates the IB model from a typical affiliate programme: the income is recurring, tied to ongoing trading activity rather than a single conversion event. See our [IB rebate tiers](/ib-rebates) for exactly how rebate rates scale with volume.

## Who typically becomes an Introducing Broker

The IB model works best for people who already have the trust of an audience interested in trading or financial markets:

- **Trading educators and course creators** who teach strategy, technical analysis or risk management
- **Community and Discord/Telegram channel owners** with an engaged trading-focused audience
- **Signal providers** who already share trade ideas and want a monetisation path beyond subscriptions
- **Financial content creators and influencers** covering forex, CFDs or commodities
- **Existing traders** with a network of friends, colleagues or followers who trade
- **Affiliate marketers** moving into financial services who want a recurring rather than one-off commission structure

None of these require a licence to become an IB — most reputable brokers require only an application and basic KYC checks, not a financial services qualification. That said, being transparent with your audience about the nature of the relationship (that you earn a rebate from their trading) is both good practice and, in many jurisdictions, a compliance expectation.

## How Introducing Brokers get paid

Rebates are usually calculated **per lot traded** — a lot being a standard unit of trading volume — rather than as a percentage of profit or loss. This distinction matters: an IB earns on trading *activity*, not on whether the client's trades are profitable. It removes any incentive to encourage reckless trading, since the rebate is the same whether a trade wins or loses.

Most programmes group IBs into **tiers** based on the total monthly volume their referred clients generate, with higher tiers unlocking a higher rebate per lot. Settlement frequency varies by broker — some pay monthly, others (including Equity IB) settle **daily**, which matters if you're relying on this income to fund ongoing business costs like advertising or content production.

## Is it worth becoming an IB?

For someone who already has an engaged trading-adjacent audience, the IB model is close to a natural monetisation layer that doesn't require building or selling a separate product. The realistic ceiling depends entirely on the trading volume your audience generates — a small, highly engaged community of active traders can outperform a much larger, passive following.

If you're weighing this against a straightforward affiliate arrangement, worth reading through the differences in payment structure and longevity before deciding — the two models solve different problems.
